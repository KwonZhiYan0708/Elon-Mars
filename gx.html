Level 1 · A name from a kid

A girl looked at the stars

Sofi Collis, age 9, from ArizonaNearly 10,000 essays in the contestHer names: Spirit and Opportunity

In 2003, NASA and the LEGO company held a contest to name two Mars rovers. In her essay, Sofi wrote that the sparkly night sky made her feel better and made her dream she could fly there. Her winning words became the names of two robots.

Who is Opportunity?
A robot geologist on Mars, about the size of a golf cart, with six wheels, solar panels and cameras for eyes. She launched in July 2003 and landed in January 2004, bouncing in airbags like a beach ball into a small crater called Eagle Crater.
SCIENCE
Orbiters had spotted a mineral called hematite on Meridiani Planum. On Earth it often forms near water, so NASA sent her there to ask: was Mars once wet?

Level 2 · The long road

Hard roads are okay

Opportunity faced many hard times. Tap each stop to see how she kept going.

NASA's plan: about 3 monthsWhat happened: about 14 years

She worked more than 50 times longer than planned. A baby born the day she landed would be 14 years old when she went quiet.

SCIENCE
NASA counts time on Mars in sols. One sol is one Mars day, about 24 hours 40 minutes. NASA planned 90 sols (about 3 months) and she worked for 5,111 sols. Wind gusts and dust devils sometimes blew dust off her solar panels, which helped a lot.
Start: Eagle CraterFinish: Endeavour Crater
About 110 laps of a 400 m school trackAbout 430 football fields
SCIENCE
At Endeavour she found clay minerals, which form in gentler water than the acidic water seen earlier. On the way she also found Heat Shield Rock, the first meteorite ever identified on another planet.
Parked on a slope that faces the sunFrost and freezing nights

Winter nights on Mars are far colder than a freezer, and the sunlight gets weak. Her team on Earth kept solving problems for her.

SCIENCE
Engineers parked her on north-facing slopes in winter so her panels caught more sunlight. When a front wheel stopped steering well they drove her backward for years, and they even changed how she used her memory when it began to fail.

Science stop · Was Mars wet?

Imagine Mars with water

Opportunity found clues that liquid water once covered places on Mars. Travel back in time and see how scientists imagine it.

Mars today: cold, dry and dusty.

Real clues from Opportunity's cameras

Blueberries up close
Tiny round balls of hematite, only 3 cm wide in this view. They grew slowly in rock soaked with water, like pearls forming.Real photo. NASA/JPL-Caltech/Cornell/USGS
The Berry Bowl rock
In this rock the balls sit inside it like berries in a bowl. Studying it helped scientists test the idea that the hematite formed in water.Real photo. NASA/JPL/Cornell
Blueberries everywhere
The same grey balls cover the plains outside the crater too. So the water story was big, not just in one small place.Real photo. NASA/JPL/Cornell
MORE CLUES
Opportunity also found rock layers shaped like ripples, which show water once flowed over sand, and salty sulfate minerals, which are left behind when salty water dries up, just like the salt left after seawater dries.
Level 3 · The great dust storm

The sky went dark

June 2018: last contactFeb 2019: mission complete

In 2018, a dust storm grew until it covered the whole planet. Sunlight could not reach Opportunity's panels. The team sent wake-up messages again and again. She never answered.

SCIENCE
You may have seen the line "My battery is low and it's getting dark". It was a poetic paraphrase by a journalist, not a real transmission.
What Opportunity made possible
Opportunity: solar panelsCuriosity (2012): nuclear battery, tall mastPerseverance (2021): nuclear battery, helicopter friend

She showed that Mars once had water. That helped scientists choose where Curiosity and Perseverance should go, and it keeps the dream of bringing Mars rocks to Earth alive. Opportunity was abandoned, but never forgotten.
